What the rank is

Half league model, half outside-information anchor. The model takes season projections and rescores them for this league: half PPR, 0.75 per catch for tight ends (0.5 baseline plus your 0.25 premium), interceptions at −2, and yardage bonuses of +3 for a 100–199 yard game and +4 for 200+ (+3 at 300–399 and +4 at 400+ passing). Those tiers are exclusive: a 210-yard game pays 4, not 7. Kickers use normal scoring (3 under 40, 4 from 40–49, 5 from 50+). Those points are measured against replacement level for your 12-team roster — QB, 2RB, 2WR, TE, RB/WR/TE flex, WR/TE flex, K, DST — so value is scarcity-adjusted rather than raw points.

The three lanes

Market answers when the room will draft him: FFC 12-team half-PPR ADP leads, with Yahoo half-PPR ADP alongside it. Expert is RotoBaller's free, expert-authored half-PPR overall Top 100. Sentiment is FantasyCalc's format-exact redraft value and trend. The outside anchor is 55% market, 30% expert and 15% sentiment, then blended 50/50 with this league's model. ESPN PPR ADP is shown only inside player details as useful platform context; it does not enter the half-PPR rank.

Why it isn't pure model

Projections can be wrong about role, health, or touchdowns. The outside anchor keeps one projection disagreement from taking over the board, while the Model column keeps the league's TE premium and bonuses visible. Edge is the outside anchor minus this board's rank: positive means this board likes the player earlier than the room and expert signals do.

Tiers

Bands are drops in value, not even buckets. Viewing everyone shows overall tiers; filtering to a position switches the bands to that position's own tiers, which is what matters when you're deciding whether to take the last back in a tier or wait a round.

Known soft spots

The model doesn't simulate role changes, so a backup is valued at his projected volume rather than what he'd get if the starter went down — spend late picks on handcuffs regardless of what the board says. Deep bench players are smoothed across 17 games. And the board inherits its projection source's opinions; where you disagree, override it.

Dad's scoring profile

This view rescores the same projections for the supplied rules: no ordinary PPR, weekly passing-yard points every 75 yards, rushing points every 25, receiving points every 20, position-specific reception buckets, distance-based touchdowns, low-value field goals, half-point PATs, and the supplied defensive scoring. It uses the Dad roster: QB, 2RB, 2WR, TE, two RB/WR/TE flexes, K, DST, and eight bench.

What has to be estimated

Season projections do not include weekly distributions or touchdown distance. Weekly yardage and catch buckets are estimated with 40,000 simulated games per player. Touchdown-distance points start from league-average distributions, tilted by yards per catch or carry for receiving and rushing scores. QB passing scores keep the league average; QB rushing scores use a short-distance tilt. These are estimates, not a player-specific touchdown-distance forecast. Defensive fumble recoveries proxy for forced fumbles because the feeds do not project forced fumbles.

How the rank is blended

The custom model carries 65% of the final rank because public boards are half-PPR and do not match these rules. The Dad Market number is the median of FFC, Yahoo, and FantasyCalc half-PPR signals, so one platform outlier cannot dominate it. That market consensus is combined with the independent expert board for the remaining 35%, preserving useful information about role, injuries, and draft timing. Kicker and defense still stay in the final rounds.

League size

This profile is calibrated for the confirmed 10-team league. Replacement level fills all ten starting lineups, and the late-position floors follow an 18-round, 180-pick draft.
